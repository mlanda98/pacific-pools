Local Knowledge

Pool Conditions in Lodi

Like most of San Joaquin County, Lodi has hard water — high in dissolved calcium and magnesium from the valley's groundwater sources. This is the leading cause of the white calcium scale buildup we see on Lodi pool tile, the rough texture that develops on older plaster surfaces, and the discoloration that appears along the waterline over time. Lodi pools also experience significant thermal cycling through the year — cold winters followed by hot, dry summers — which stresses plaster surfaces and coping materials. Older white plaster in Lodi pools tends to show its age faster than in cooler climates for this reason. When we remodel or replaster a Lodi pool, we account for both the local water chemistry and the climate, recommending durable finishes and proper mastic work to protect expansion joints from seasonal ground movement.

Why is my Lodi pool plaster rough and stained?

Hard water is the most common cause in Lodi. Local water is high in calcium and magnesium, which causes mineral scaling on tile and surface deterioration on older plaster. Quartz or pebble finishes hold up significantly better in these conditions.
