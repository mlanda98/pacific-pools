Local Water Quality

Pool Water Conditions in Newman, CA

Water Hardness: ~195 ppm (11.4 GPG) — Very Hard Source: groundwater from the western San Joaquin Valley aquifer, similar profile to Los Banos

Newman, like neighboring Los Banos, draws from the harder groundwater of the western San Joaquin Valley. Water hardness typically runs in the very hard range — 11+ GPG — due to the mineral-rich geology of the Coast Range foothills. Newman pool owners deal with the same aggressive calcium scaling and accelerated plaster deterioration as homeowners in the harder-water cities of Stanislaus County. When we work on a Newman pool, we treat it with the same attention to water chemistry as a Modesto remodel — recommending denser surfaces and ensuring all tile and coping work holds up to the local mineral content.

Local Climate

Newman Climate & Your Pool

Avg. July High: 97°F | Avg. Winter Low: 40°F

Newman sits in the western San Joaquin Valley with a hot summer climate similar to Los Banos — July highs around 97°F with peak days frequently reaching 104°F. The city is predominantly agricultural with very little summer rainfall. Winters bring cool temperatures in the low 40s.

Newman's combination of very hard western valley groundwater and intense summer heat creates similar pool surface challenges to Los Banos. High evaporation in summer concentrates minerals, the thermal cycling stresses finishes and expansion joints, and the agricultural soil profile means seasonal ground movement is a factor. For a Newman pool remodel to hold up over the long term, all three need to be addressed: the right finish for local water chemistry, proper mastic installation, and a surface preparation process that doesn't cut corners.
