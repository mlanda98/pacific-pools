Local Water Quality

Pool Water Conditions in Clovis, CA

Water Hardness: ~115 ppm (6.7 GPG) — Moderately Hard Source: groundwater and imported surface water, similar to Fresno

Clovis shares a similar water profile with neighboring Fresno — moderately hard water around 6-7 GPG from a blended supply of groundwater and surface water. While less aggressive than the very hard water in Stanislaus County, Clovis pools still develop calcium deposits on tile and experience gradual plaster wear. The extreme summer heat — Clovis is one of the hottest cities in the Valley — puts significant thermal stress on pool surfaces, meaning older plaster finishes deteriorate faster than their rated lifespan even with moderate water hardness. A quartz or pebble finish handles both factors better than standard white plaster.

Local Climate

Clovis Climate & Your Pool

Avg. July High: 99°F | Avg. Winter Low: 40°F

Clovis has one of the most intense summer climates in the state — July highs average 99°F and peak days regularly exceed 107–110°F. The city gets virtually no rain from May through September and has long, brutally hot summers. Winters are cool with lows around 40°F and tule fog.

Clovis is one of the fastest-growing cities in California, and with that growth has come thousands of newer pool installations that will start needing replastering and remodeling as they age. The extreme summer heat is the dominant factor in pool surface wear here — high evaporation rates concentrate minerals quickly, and the thermal stress on plaster and coping from near-freezing winter nights to 107°F summer days causes surface degradation over time. When we remodel a Clovis pool, heat tolerance is a key criterion in the finish recommendation.
