Local Water Quality

Pool Water Conditions in Hanford, CA

Water Hardness: ~150 ppm (8.8 GPG) — Hard Source: groundwater from the Kings River alluvial aquifer beneath Kings County

Hanford draws from the Kings River alluvial aquifer, with water hardness around 8-9 GPG. Calcium scaling on pool tile is a common complaint among Hanford pool owners, and the mineral content accelerates wear on standard plaster surfaces over time. Kings County sits on some of the most productive agricultural land in California, and intensive irrigation over decades has concentrated minerals in the shallow groundwater. When we remodel a Hanford pool, we strip calcium deposits from existing tile, repair the shell as needed, and recommend finishes that are denser and more resistant to mineral etching than basic white plaster.

Local Climate

Hanford Climate & Your Pool

Avg. July High: 99°F | Avg. Winter Low: 39°F

Hanford is one of the hotter cities in the San Joaquin Valley — July highs average 99°F with peak days regularly reaching 107–110°F. The city sits on the flat Kings County floor with no topographical features to moderate the heat. Winters bring cool temperatures, lows around 39°F, and valley fog.

Hanford's extreme heat is a major factor in pool surface wear alongside the hard water — high evaporation rates during summer concentrate minerals in the pool water faster than the water hardness number alone would suggest, and the thermal stress from hot summers to cool winters puts repeated pressure on plaster finishes and expansion joints. At 99°F average July highs, a pool in Hanford is working harder than one in a cooler city, and standard white plaster reflects that in its shorter service life. Denser surfaces and properly sealed expansion joints are essential for a Hanford remodel that holds up.
