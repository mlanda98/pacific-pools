Local Water Quality

Pool Water Conditions in Riverbank, CA

Water Hardness: ~185 ppm (10.8 GPG) — Very Hard Source: groundwater from the Stanislaus River corridor aquifer

Riverbank sits along the Stanislaus River, and while river proximity might suggest softer water, Riverbank's municipal supply still comes from groundwater that tests in the very hard range — around 10-11 GPG. The city's pools show typical Central Valley hard water signatures: calcium deposits on tile, rough plaster texture as surfaces age, and accelerated deterioration compared to areas with softer water. Riverbank pools are also affected by the expansive clay soils common along river corridors — seasonal soil movement can stress coping and mastic joints over time, which is why we pay close attention to expansion joint work on every Riverbank remodel.

Local Climate

Riverbank Climate & Your Pool

Avg. July High: 97°F | Avg. Winter Low: 40°F

Riverbank has the same hot valley climate as surrounding Stanislaus County cities — July highs average 97°F with peak summer days frequently reaching 103–106°F. Almost no rain falls between June and September. Winters are cool with lows around 40°F and valley fog common from December through February.

The Stanislaus River corridor doesn't provide any cooling relief for Riverbank pools in summer — the heat is still intense, evaporation rates are high, and the mineral concentration effect from evaporation compounds the existing hard water issue. The river proximity does mean clay-heavy soils in some parts of Riverbank that expand when wet in winter and contract when dry in summer — this movement is a primary cause of the coping separation and mastic joint failure we see in older Riverbank pools.
